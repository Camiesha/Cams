What this document is

This is the single source of truth. It audits the earlier reports, resolves their inconsistencies, locks one formula, and recalculates every example against it. Where the earlier drafts differ, this document wins. All weights/thresholds are proposed, pending validation; no conversion rate, probability, or CRM result is invented; sourced claims are cited with their type and caveats.

01Executive summary

The model scores what the customer does, not how complete their record is; it updates automatically; and it keeps six ideas apart — priority score, classification, pipeline stage, follow-up urgency, data readiness, and (eventually) conversion probability.

  • Hybrid rules model — weighted points + milestone logic + classification overrides + lifecycle states. Most explainable, and honest at a two-venue data scale; predictive ML is deferred to Phase 6.
  • Locked 100-point split: Buying intent ≤55 · Fit ≤35 · Engagement ≤10, minus a capped, reversible negative pool ≤25, clamped to 0–100.
  • Two corrections this version makes beyond the prior drafts: (1) a deliberate distinction between a confirmed tour and an unconfirmed tour request — only a confirmed/held tour floors a lead at Warm; (2) explicit dormancy handling so a high-scoring lead that goes silent cools via decay, not a reclassification, while new leads are never penalised for thin data.
  • Data completeness is tracked separately as confidence/readiness and never adds buying-intent points.
  • The 0–100 figure is a priority ranking, not a booking probability — §06 defines the prediction window and what must be true before any percentage likelihood is shown.

03Scope & current-state limitations

The current CRM score largely rewards profile completeness and staff admin actions, conflating a complete profile with a strong buying signal, and merging score, stage, urgency and data-readiness into one number. This spec separates them and makes behaviour the dominant input. It defines logic, rules, triggers, tests and a roadmap; it contains no CRM screens, card designs, wireframes, prototypes or code — only research and logic visuals.

04Research methodology & verified evidence

Claims are tagged by source type. Two figures the prior drafts leaned on were re-verified this round; both are vendor-reported, not universal.

  • Fit + behaviour on two axes, read together — durable pattern; neither axis alone suffices.65 (practitioner/industry)
  • Behaviour is the dynamic predictor; decay old activity.14 (vendor)
  • Explainability via top positive/negative factors.3 (vendor docs)
  • Speed to respond matters — HBR 2011 (Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington): contacting within an hour ≈7× the odds of a qualifying conversation, on a ~1.25M-lead dataset.7 A separate 2007 InsideSales/MIT-Sloan presentation (MarketingSherpa) reported ≈21× qualifying odds at 5 vs 30 minutes — vendor-linked, not peer-reviewed, and relative odds, not conversion rates.8
  • Predictive scoring needs volume — a widely-repeated vendor threshold for Salesforce Einstein is ≈1,000 leads with ≈120 conversions, but the window varies by source (180 days / 6 / 12 months) and it was not located on official Salesforce help; treat as order-of-magnitude, not a universal rule.2
  • Accessibility: never colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1); contrast 1.4.3 / 1.4.11.9 (W3C standard)

06Priority score vs. conversion probability

Priority score (now)

A rules-based number 0–100 ranking leads by buying intent, fit and engagement. It answers "who should a salesperson work first?" It is ordinal — an 85 outranks a 50 — and is not a percentage chance of booking.

Conversion probability (later)

A statistically estimated likelihood of a confirmed booking (paid deposit or signed agreement) within a defined observation window of the inquiry. Requires a fitted, validated, calibrated model.

Define the target before any probability is shown: outcome = confirmed booking; proposed window = within 180 days of inquiry (set to your actual median inquiry-to-event lead time from data — a parameter, not a fact). To legitimately display a percentage: (1) enough closed outcomes across both venues; (2) a model whose predicted probabilities match observed booking rates (calibration curve); (3) monitoring for drift. Until all three hold, the UI shows a priority score and never a "%". The vendor order-of-magnitude for predictive readiness (~1,000 leads/120 conversions) is a reference point, not a target to force.2

Hard rule

A priority score of 70 is never labelled "70% chance of booking." No calibration result, probability, or historical rate is asserted in this document.

11Prioritization & explainability

Follow-up urgency is computed separately from the score — a work-queue signal, not a classification. Drivers: new inquiry awaiting first response, a just-booked or approaching tour, a completed tour awaiting a quote, an active deposit discussion, an overdue follow-up task, a newly-dormant previously-warm lead. A lead can be Hot and overdue (Ex. 11), or new-and-thin yet top of the queue (Ex. 3). No single blended number is proposed — urgency and score answer different questions and are shown as separate cues.

Explainability: each lead stores and displays its top positive and negative factors in plain language plus the latest change, e.g. (hypothetical) "+25 confirmed a tour" as a positive signal and, separately, "follow-up 3 days overdue" as an urgency item (not a score penalty).3

Recency & dormancy are handled by decay of the engagement pool — never by treating silence as a negative for a new or never-contacted lead. For a previously engaged lead, prolonged silence (≥3 unanswered follow-ups) applies the capped, reversible −8 and raises a Dormant flag; the lead cools via decay toward its underlying fit+intent, not to Cold by silence alone, and recovers fully on any reply (Ex. 10).4

★Two summaries, and what to decide

For sales leadership

The score ranks who to call first by real buying intent, not by who filled in the most fields. A confirmed tour or a deposit question lifts a lead immediately; an unconfirmed tour request is chased hard but not over-rated; silence cools a lead gently and reversibly; a booking leaves the queue. Every lead shows why and what next; new leads are flagged for a fast first response, never buried. The number is a priority ranking, not odds of booking, until proven against real bookings.

For the CRM dev team

Build one server-side, deterministic, idempotent scoring function (§07), apply the precedence tree (§08), recompute on §10 triggers + nightly decay, persist per-factor contributions + audit log, keep weights in versioned config. Confirm the data sources flagged below, validate against the §15 suite on staging, ship in the §17 phases.

Leadership must approve

  1. The 100-pt split (55/35/10) and bands (70/40/0).
  2. The confirmed-vs-unconfirmed tour distinction and the Warm/Hot floors.
  3. The 180-day conversion-window definition (set from your lead times).
  4. Treating completeness as confidence, not score.
  5. Deposit/signature = conversion exit (Won), not Hot.
  6. The quarterly review cadence.

Developers must verify against the schema

  1. Tour source flag (Calendly self-serve vs. staff-booked).
  2. Tour attendance and no-show capture.
  3. Message direction & reliable timestamps.
  4. A quote event (requested/sent/revised).
  5. Payment and e-signature hooks.
  6. Where per-factor contributions & the audit log will be stored.

✓Final consistency audit

  • Point allocations sum within caps (fit 6+10+8+5+6=35; intent base ≤52 +bonuses capped 55; eng 6+4=10; neg ≤25).
  • Classification ranges contiguous, no gaps/overlaps (0–39 / 40–69 / 70–100).
  • Overrides have explicit precedence (1 Won → 2 Lost/DQ → 3 Hot floor → 4 Warm floor → 5 Not scored → 6 band) and reset conditions.
  • Unknown information is neutral (0), never negative.
  • A confirmed self-booked tour yields at least Warm; an unconfirmed request deliberately does not.
  • A deposit discussion triggers Hot (subject to lifecycle/DQ).
  • Related milestones replace, not accumulate; duplicates are idempotent (Ex. 14).
  • Decay and negative-signal recovery are reversible and consistent (Ex. 8, 10).
  • All 15 examples follow the one formula; totals verified.
  • Score always clamped to 0–100.
  • Converted leads exit ordinary prioritization (Won).
  • Follow-up urgency is independent of the score (Ex. 11).
  • Source claims are cited with type; the two re-verified stats carry caveats.
  • No illustrative figure is presented as a measured business result; no probability is asserted.
  • Proposed rules are distinguished from verified facts and from unconfirmed CRM capabilities.
