Lead Scoring — Final Specification

Crown Prince Event Hall (CPH, ≤300) & Alvin Studio & Courtyard (ACD, ≤100)

Replace completeness-based scoring with a behaviour-driven system that identifies high-intent leads, prioritises follow-ups, and avoids overlooking promising inquiries.

1Objective

Replace the current completeness-based scoring with a behaviour-driven lead-scoring system that helps the sales team identify high-intent leads, prioritise follow-ups, and avoid overlooking promising inquiries.

2Scoring formula

Priority Score = Fit (35) + Buying Intent (55) + Engagement (10) − Negative Signals (up to 25)
clamp(score, 0, 100)
Fit 35

Event type, date, guest capacity, availability, and budget compatibility.

Buying intent 55

Tour activity, quotation requests, and deposit discussions.

Engagement 10

Recent customer replies and interaction frequency.

Negative signals −25

No-shows, repeated rescheduling, and prolonged silence after prior engagement.

Data completeness: tracked separately — missing information must not reduce the score.

All weights are proposed and require validation against actual CRM outcomes.

3Classification rules

ClassificationScoreKey rule
○Cold0–39Low current priority
◐Warm40–69Moderate intent or confirmed tour
●Hot70–100Strong buying intent
◇Not scored yet—Insufficient information
✓Won—Deposit received or agreement signed
✕Lost—Disqualified or explicitly uninterested

Required overrides:

  • A confirmed tour immediately qualifies for at least Warm.
  • An unconfirmed tour request signals intent but does not automatically qualify for Warm.
  • Asking about a deposit or securing a date triggers Hot.
  • Disqualification and explicit disinterest override the score.
  • Won and Lost leads leave the ordinary prospect queue.
  • New leads must not be classified as Cold simply because their records are incomplete.

4Automation requirements

Implement one server-side scoring function as the single source of truth for desktop and mobile. Recalculate scores when:

  • A lead is created or customer details change.
  • A tour is booked, rescheduled, cancelled, attended, or missed.
  • A customer replies.
  • A quotation is requested or sent.
  • A deposit is discussed, received, or an agreement is signed.
  • Engagement becomes stale, requiring decay or dormancy evaluation.

Prevent duplicate events from changing scores twice. Record score changes, contributing factors, timestamps, and correction history. Negative signals must be reversible when appropriate.

5Separate follow-up urgency

Do not combine urgency with the priority score. Prioritise new inquiries awaiting first contact, upcoming tours, post-tour quotations, active deposit discussions, overdue tasks, and previously engaged leads that have gone dormant.

Display the score, classification, reasons for the score, missing information, and recommended next action.

6Validation & acceptance tests

Before launch, verify that:

  • Confirmed tours and deposit inquiries trigger the correct classifications.
  • Missing data does not unfairly lower scores.
  • Milestones replace earlier milestones instead of double-counting them.
  • Duplicate events are ignored.
  • Corrections and negative-signal recovery work correctly.
  • Follow-up urgency remains independent of scoring.
  • Payments and signed agreements transition leads to Won.
  • Desktop and mobile display consistent results.

Test the rules against historical CRM data before adjusting weights and thresholds. Treat conversion probability as a separate future feature requiring sufficient data and statistical validation.

7Implementation priorities

  1. Audit existing CRM fields, integrations, and event tracking.
  2. Approve the scoring matrix, thresholds, and override rules.
  3. Build the scoring engine, audit log, and automation.
  4. Test against acceptance criteria before rollout.
  5. Validate and refine using actual booking outcomes.
Important

The 0–100 score represents lead priority, not the percentage chance of booking. All proposed weights and thresholds remain provisional until validated against real outcomes.
