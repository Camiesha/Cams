Lead Scoring — Build Specification

Crown Prince Event Hall (CPH, ≤300) & Alvin Studio & Courtyard (ACD, ≤100)

A practical scoring system sales leadership can approve and a developer can build — one table, clear rules, automatic updates. Buying behaviour drives the classification, not profile completeness.

What the number is

The 0–100 score is a priority score that ranks which leads to work first. The percentage bands below are provisional scoring bands, not measured booking probabilities. Real conversion probabilities come later, from booking history (see §6). All weights are proposed and should be tuned once outcomes accrue.

2The scoring matrix

Score = Fit + Buying Intent + Engagement − Negative Signals, clamped to 0–100. Buying Intent uses the highest milestone reached (it does not add milestones together, so one tour isn't counted twice). Unknown fields score 0 — never negative.

3Behaviour-driven rules (overrides)

Applied after the score, in this order — the first that matches wins:

  1. Won — deposit paid or agreement signed → leaves the prospect queue.
  2. Lost — explicit disinterest, or requirements fit neither venue → Cold, overrides any score.
  3. Deposit discussion → Hot, regardless of the number.
  4. Confirmed Calendly tour (or attended tour) → at least Warm, even if budget, guest count and everything else are missing.
  5. Too little info → Not scored yet (with the missing fields), never Cold.
  6. Otherwise, classify by score (70 / 40 thresholds).
The signature case

A customer submits an inquiry and immediately books a Calendly tour: +28 intent, and rule 4 makes them Warm on the spot — even with no budget or guest count yet. An unconfirmed request for a tour signals interest but does not force Warm; it is chased hard as a follow-up instead.

4Automatic updates

The score and classification recalculate automatically — on the server, so desktop and mobile always match — whenever:

  • A new inquiry arrives, or lead details are added or corrected.
  • A Calendly tour is booked, rescheduled, cancelled, attended, or missed.
  • A customer replies (or goes silent — a nightly check applies decay).
  • A quote is requested or sent.
  • A deposit is discussed, a deposit is received, or an agreement is signed.

Requirements: one scoring function as the single source of truth; ignore duplicate events (same Calendly booking counts once); keep an audit log of score changes with the reason; negative signals are reversible when the customer re-engages. Each lead shows its score, classification, the top reasons, what's missing, and the recommended next action. Follow-up urgency is tracked separately from the score — a Hot lead can still have an overdue task, and a brand-new lead can need an urgent first reply.

6Priority score now, real probability later

Today's bands are a priority ranking — useful immediately and easy to explain. They are not a measured chance of booking, and must never be shown as one (a score of 70 is not "70% likely to book").

Once the CRM has accumulated enough booked vs. not-booked history, actual probabilities can be calculated — and this is simple to start:

  1. Record each lead's outcome: did it become a confirmed booking, and when.
  2. Compute the historical booking rate for each segment — e.g. "of leads that attended a tour, X% went on to book." That rate is a real, data-grounded probability.
  3. Show it only where the sample is large enough to be meaningful; otherwise keep showing the priority score. Later, a predictive model can refine these rates per venue.

Keep the two clearly separate in the interface: the priority score (now) and the booking probability (later, labelled as such only when it is real).

7Implementation priorities

  1. First  Confirm the CRM records each signal in the table — especially Calendly booking source, tour attendance/no-show, replies with timestamps, quote events, and deposit/agreement events. Flag any gaps.
  2. First  Build the server-side scoring function, the six override rules, and the "Not scored yet" state. Backfill existing leads.
  3. Next  Add automatic recalculation on every event + a nightly decay job; dedupe duplicate events; keep the audit log.
  4. Next  Show score, classification, top reasons, missing info, and next action on the lead; keep follow-up urgency separate.
  5. Later  Once booking outcomes accrue, tune the weights and thresholds, then introduce segment booking rates (real probabilities) per §6.
Decisions for leadership

Approve: (1) the weights and the 70/40 thresholds; (2) the rule that a confirmed Calendly tour = Warm and a deposit discussion = Hot; (3) treating missing info as neutral and new leads as "Not scored yet"; (4) that the number is a priority score, not a booking probability, until §6 is in place.
