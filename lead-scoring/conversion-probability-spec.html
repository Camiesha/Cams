Conversion-probability specification · October 2026

From Priority Score to Booking Probability

A data-driven system that estimates each lead's actual likelihood of becoming a confirmed booking — kept strictly separate from the rules-based priority score, and shown only when it is statistically earned.

CPH (≤300) & ACD (≤100) · extends the final rules spec · no probability is asserted without a validated, calibrated model
The one rule that governs this document

A priority score of 70 is not a 70% chance of booking. The rules-based score (0–100) ranks who to work first; the conversion probability is a statistically estimated %, produced only by a model trained and calibrated on CPH/ACD's own booking outcomes. Until that model exists and passes validation, the CRM shows the priority score and no percentage. No probability, conversion rate, or accuracy figure is invented anywhere in this spec.

01Executive summary

  • Two outputs, never merged. (A) Priority score + Hot/Warm/Cold — rules-based, available now, from the existing final spec; (B) Conversion probability — model-based, phased in as data accrues.
  • Define the target before any model. Outcome = a confirmed booking (paid reservation deposit or signed agreement); prediction window = a data-derived horizon from historical inquiry-to-deposit times, not a guessed number (§05).
  • A maturity ladder, honest at each rung: (0) reference-class base rates → (1) calibrated logistic regression → (2) gradient-boosted trees — each wrapped in probability calibration and gated by data volume.1
  • Validate without leakage: out-of-time (train-past / test-future) splits, preprocessing fit per fold, features restricted to what is known at scoring time; judge by calibration (reliability, Brier) as well as discrimination (AUC).3
  • Sample size is context-dependent, not a universal threshold — the classic ~10-events-per-predictor rule is a starting heuristic, challenged by later work; use few features and penalised regression when data is thin.2
  • Insufficient data → no fabricated %. Fall back to the priority score, or at most a clearly-labelled reference-class rate marked "indicative, small sample."
  • Business rule preserved: a confirmed self-booked tour is Warm immediately (classification), independent of whatever probability the model later assigns; missing data stays neutral.

03Research & sources

Evidence tagged by type; vendor figures are not treated as universal. Verified facts vs. recommendations are distinguished throughout.

  • Probability calibration — map raw model outputs to true frequencies: Platt (sigmoid) for small data; isotonic only with ample data (scikit-learn warns isotonic overfits with <<1000 samples); calibrate on data not used to fit the model.1 (official docs)
  • Sample size — the ~10 events-per-variable rule (Peduzzi 1996) is a heuristic; later work (van Smeden 2016, Ogundimu 2016, Riley 2019) shows it is context-dependent and often favours penalised methods.2 (peer-reviewed)
  • Leakage-free validation — train on earlier data, test on later; rolling/expanding windows; fit scalers/imputers/encoders per fold; use only features available at prediction time; compare random vs. time splits to detect leakage.3 (practitioner + docs)
  • Behaviour > completeness; decay old activity.6 Fit and engagement on two axes.7 (vendor/industry)
  • Speed-to-lead — HBR 2011 ≈7× qualifying odds within an hour; the 2007 InsideSales/MIT "21×" is a vendor-linked presentation, not peer-reviewed; both are relative odds, not conversion rates.8
  • Predictive-readiness vendor yardstick — Salesforce Einstein's ≈1,000 leads/120 conversions is vendor-reported, window varies, not found on official help; reference only.5

04Two separate outputs

Output A — available now

Priority score & classification

Rules-based 0–100 (Fit 35 + Intent 55 + Engagement 10 − Negatives ≤25) → Hot 70–100 · Warm 40–69 · Cold 0–39, with overrides (confirmed tour → Warm; deposit → Hot; Won/Lost exits).

Is: a priority ranking. Is not: a probability. Never printed with "%". Specified in final-specification.html.

Output B — phased in

Conversion probability

A model-estimated P(confirmed booking within the window), 0–100%, from CPH/ACD outcomes, calibrated and validated.

Shown only when the model passes calibration + out-of-time validation for the relevant venue/segment. Otherwise hidden; the priority score stands in.

The two can disagree and that is expected: a lead can be high priority (worth a call now) yet modest probability (early in a long sales cycle), or vice-versa. They answer different questions — "who first?" vs. "how likely?" — and are displayed as distinct fields. Follow-up urgency remains a third, separate signal.

05Conversion probability — outcome & prediction window

Outcome label

y = 1 if the lead reaches Won — a paid reservation deposit or a signed agreement, whichever the business treats as the commitment point — within the prediction window of the inquiry date; y = 0 otherwise. Lost/disqualified leads are y = 0. Leads whose window has not yet fully elapsed are right-censored: excluded from the binary training set, or modelled with survival methods (below). Define one commitment point and apply it consistently so labels are unambiguous.

Prediction window — derive it, don't guess

Event-venue lead times are long and vary by event type (a wedding booked 9–14 months out differs from a birthday booked in weeks). So the window is a parameter computed from history, not a fixed number:

  • Compute the distribution of inquiry → deposit intervals from past bookings, per event type and venue.
  • Set the window at a high percentile (e.g. the point by which the large majority of eventual deposits have historically landed). State it explicitly, e.g. W = ⟨p90 of inquiry→deposit, from data⟩.
  • Because horizons are long and censoring is heavy, the more rigorous option is a discrete-time hazard / survival model (probability of booking by horizon t), which uses censored leads correctly instead of discarding them. Recommended once volume allows; the binary-within-W model is the simpler start.
Why this matters

A fixed 180-day window would mislabel a still-live 10-month wedding lead as a non-booking. Deriving W from real lead times (or using survival framing) is what makes the probability defensible rather than arbitrary.

06Recommended model & required data

Required historical data

  • Labels: per lead, did it reach Won within W (and the deposit/agreement date) — to compute y and lead times.
  • Point-in-time features: each event with a reliable timestamp, so features can be reconstructed as known at any scoring moment (no future information).
  • Venue & event type on every lead, for per-venue/segment modelling and the window.
  • Enough events (the minority class = bookings) — judged by context, not a fixed cutoff (§09).

Anti-leakage & anti-double-count: every feature must be reconstructable from events dated at or before the scoring moment; correlated events (a booking and its automated confirmation) collapse to one state; unknown fields are encoded as an explicit "unknown" category, never imputed as a bad value.

08CRM automation & backend

When probability updates: each event produces a new feature snapshot → a fresh P from the live model; the value and its inputs are logged. Converted leads (Won) have a realised outcome — show "Booked", not a prediction — and exit prospect prioritisation. If the model is not live for a lead's venue/segment, the probability field reads "not yet available" and the priority score is shown instead.

09Validation, calibration & sample size

Sample size — context, not a cutoff

The ≈10-events-per-predictor heuristic is a starting point, challenged as a fixed rule; higher EPV or penalised regression is advised when bookings are rare, and modern guidance favours performance-based sizing.2 Practically: begin at Rung 0 (base rates need only counts), keep features few, penalise, and climb rungs per venue only when out-of-time calibration holds. Treat any single threshold (including the Salesforce vendor figure5) as context-dependent.

Per venue & bias

Validate CPH and ACD separately where samples allow; otherwise pool with a venue feature and note the limitation. Watch that the model doesn't systematically under-score new or thin-data leads, or encode staff-follow-up patterns as if they were customer intent.

5

Monitor & (maybe) Rung 2 / survival

Deliverable: drift monitoring; GBM or discrete-time hazard only if it wins on calibration · Done: review cadence running

Decisions leadership must make

  1. The commitment point for "Won" (paid deposit vs. signed agreement).
  2. Acceptance of a derived prediction window (and whether to invest in survival modelling for long horizons).
  3. The decision threshold trade-off (missed booking vs. wasted effort).
  4. Whether Rung-0 segment rates may be shown while small, clearly labelled, or withheld until robust.

Developer questions to verify

  1. Are all required event timestamps reliable and immutable enough to reconstruct point-in-time features?
  2. Where will labels, feature snapshots, model versions and the audit log live?
  3. Is there a clean historical export of leads + outcomes for backtesting, per venue?
  4. How are corrections/duplicates represented so re-derivation stays leakage-free?
