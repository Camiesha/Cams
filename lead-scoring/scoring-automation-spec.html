A Buying-Intent Lead Score, Specified to Build

An evidence-based research report and a complete, internally consistent scoring specification — matrix, classification rules, automation triggers, test cases and a phased roadmap — for the Crown Prince Event Hall & Alvin Studio & Courtyard CRM.

CPH (≤300) · ACD (≤100) · research + proposed rules · every weight provisional, pending validation · no interface designed or built

01Research scope

This document answers five questions: how to present an automated score so staff act on it; how to score real buying intent rather than profile completeness; how scores and priorities should update automatically; how to validate the model against bookings; and what the development team must implement. It visualizes the research and the logic — comparison matrices, scoring breakdowns, a classification decision tree, a customer journey, an event-flow diagram, a validation loop — and deliberately contains no CRM screens, card designs, wireframes, prototypes or code.

Integrity of every number

Weights, thresholds and bands here are proposed values requiring validation against real outcomes, not established facts. Sourced claims are cited inline; everything else is labelled as our proposed rule, business judgment, or a clearly hypothetical example. No conversion rate or CRM result has been invented.

02Executive summary

The redesign rests on one correction: score what the customer did, not how complete their record is — then show that score with its reasons and a next step.

  • Recommended model — a hybrid weighted score with milestone logic, classification overrides and lifecycle states. It is the most explainable option and the most honest at the modest data volumes of a two-venue business; predictive ML is deferred until the data supports it.2
  • Six dimensions, intent dominant (proposed 100-point split: Buying intent 55 · Fit 35 · Engagement 10, with a capped, reversible negative pool of up to −25). Fit and behaviour are scored on separate axes and read together, per the evidence.6
  • Data completeness is tracked separately as a confidence / readiness measure — it never adds buying-intent points. Rewarding completeness is the current model's core defect.
  • Explainability is the highest-value feature: show the top positive and negative factors in plain language, as mature predictive tools do.3
  • Overrides guarantee the cases that matter: a self-booked Calendly tour floors a lead at Warm even with a blank profile; a deposit discussion floors it at Hot; explicit disinterest caps it at Cold; a paid deposit converts it to Won and removes it from prospect prioritisation.
  • The 0–100 figure is a priority ranking, not a probability of booking — and must never be shown as a percentage likelihood until a model is calibrated against outcomes.2

03Current-state limitations

Today's model rewards guest count, a staff phone call, event type and contact fields — largely profile completeness and administrative actions. Its structural weakness is conflating a complete profile with a strong buying signal: a lead who fills every field but takes no next step can outscore one who self-booked a tour minutes after inquiring. It also merges concepts that should stay distinct — fit, buying intent, engagement, data completeness, pipeline stage and follow-up urgency — into a single number and a single colour. The redesign separates them.

04Evidence & research method

Findings draw on CRM/sales-operations guidance, data-visualization and accessibility standards, and marketing-automation documentation. Where a source is a vendor, it is marked as vendor guidance; point values vendors quote are examples, not standards, as the sources themselves note. Primary standards (WCAG) and widely-cited studies are distinguished from practitioner guidance in §16.

  • Fit vs. behaviour on two axes, read together — the durable pattern; neither axis suffices alone.65
  • Explicit (static fit) vs. implicit (behaviour); behaviour is the dynamic predictor.1
  • Decay behavioural points so stale activity doesn't keep a dead lead hot.4
  • Speed to respond is decisive — so new leads must never be rendered low-priority for lack of data.7
  • Top positive/negative factors are how predictive tools stay explainable.3
  • Predictive scoring needs volume — Salesforce cites ~1,000 leads / 120 conversions.2
  • Accessibility: never colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1); contrast minimums (1.4.3 / 1.4.11).8

06Proposed scoring dimensions & complete matrix

Score = clamp( Fit(≤35) + Intent(≤55) + Engagement(≤10) − Negatives(≤25), 0, 100 ). Intent is a milestone ladder — the furthest milestone sets the base; it does not add across milestones — plus small one-time bonuses, the whole block capped at 55. This is what prevents double-counting related steps (a Calendly booking and its automated confirmation are one milestone, not two).

Technical requirements

  • Single source of truth — one server-side function; clients render stored values.
  • Idempotent processing — dedupe by event id; retries and duplicate webhooks apply once.
  • Recalculation on change — editing/correcting historical data re-derives the score.
  • Audit history — append-only log of every score change, delta, reason, source, time.
  • Factor storage — persist each factor's contribution (powers the explanation).
  • Timestamps & decay — reliable activity times; nightly decay job.
  • Out-of-order / corrected events — reconcile to the furthest valid milestone; deletions reverse their contribution.
  • Manual overrides (if allowed) — logged with user + reason.
  • Rules separated from presentation — weights in versioned config; UI reads results.
  • Safe deployment — test suite (§13) on a staging copy before touching live records.

09Visualization & explainability

Explainability in plain language. The record should state, in words, the strongest positive and negative drivers and the latest change — e.g. (hypothetical) "+25 self-booked a tour" as a positive signal, and separately "follow-up 3 days overdue" as an action concern (an urgency flag, not a score penalty). Staff should be able to read: why the score changed, which action caused it, which factors matter most, what is missing, and what to do next.

12Validation framework & model limits

A priority ranking is useful immediately. A probability of booking must be earned through validation — and a two-venue business may not have the volume for a trustworthy predictive model for some time.

The honesty test

The score becomes a probability only after predicted likelihoods match observed rates (calibration) — which needs volume (Salesforce cites ~1,000 leads / 120 conversions2). Two venues may take 12–24 months to approach that. Until then: label it a priority score, use the hybrid rules, and prefer the simple model over premature ML with small samples.

★Two summaries

For sales leadership

The score will rank who to call first by real buying intent, not by who filled in the most fields. A self-booked tour or a deposit question pushes a lead up immediately; silence cools it gradually; a booking removes it from the queue. Staff will see, for each lead, why it scored as it did and what to do next, with new leads flagged for a fast first response rather than buried. Treat the number as a priority ranking, not odds of booking, until it is proven against real bookings — which will take time at two venues.

For the CRM development team

Build one server-side, deterministic, idempotent scoring function (matrix §6), apply the decision tree (§7) for classification/overrides/lifecycle, and recompute on the triggers in §8 plus a nightly decay job. Persist per-factor contributions and an audit log; keep weights in versioned config, separate from presentation. First confirm the data sources flagged in §8. Validate with the §13 suite on staging before touching live records. Deliver in the §15 phases.
