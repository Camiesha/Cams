Five priority improvements

  4. State booking hours and setup time unambiguously — resolve CPH's 6-hour-vs-setup contradiction.
  5. Make fee and price disclosure transparent — reconcile ACD's advertised price with its rate sheet; publish the CPH guest-count formula.

Private banquet halls still publish nothing, so CPH-scale cancellation terms remain a design-to-the-law question.

04

Best practices & policy gaps

Grouped for decision-making. Each adoption is supported by the evidence above; none is called an "industry standard" beyond what the data shows.

Worth adopting

  • Clear booking-confirmation requirement (signed + reservation payment)
  • Separate reservation payment and refundable security deposit
  • Transparent payment schedule and cancellation calculation
  • Documented damage deductions and stated refund timelines
  • Published overtime rate and clearly defined booking hours
  • Consistent insurance and vendor-document requirements

Clarify or correct

  • CPH setup time: rate card & website agree (6-hr window + 2 complimentary setup hours); the rental agreement's generic "rental time includes setup" clause is the wording to align
  • CPH guest-count pricing / fee formula undefined above 100
  • ACD $1,000 serving multiple purposes
  • ACD advertised price vs supplied rate sheet
  • Missing overtime rates (both)
  • ACD contracting entity & payment recipient
  • Unverified courtyard capacity; BYO-bar rules under-defined

Retain (already good)

  • CPH 14-day itemized security-deposit return
  • CPH insurance stack ($1M/$2M + host liquor, COI 30d)
  • CPH venue-bar model (keeps events licensed)
  • ACD graduated 3-step cancellation (closer to best practice)
  • ACD 1-year rescheduling credit
  • ACD prohibited-conduct & termination provisions
Why these, briefly. The adoptions close the gaps competitors reveal (refundable deposits, concrete overtime, transparent pricing) and the corrections remove the internal contradictions that cause wrong quotes and disputes. Items touching enforceability — cancellation percentages, waivers, surcharge wording, BYO-alcohol, accessibility — require California counsel; pricing and entity items require management.

Deposit vocabulary (adopt both venues). Reservation payment secures the date · Rental payment pays for the booking · Refundable security deposit covers post-event obligations · Rescheduling credit a defined credit with an expiry. The ACD $1,000 should resolve into these, not remain one amount with four jobs.
Late fee on past-due balances (added). After the final-balance due date, apply a flat, reasonable late fee — a set dollar amount or a modest percentage of the overdue balance — only after written notice and a short cure window, before any cancellation. Keep it defensible: California treats a periodic percentage "interest" charge (e.g. 1%/month = 12%/yr) as risky under usury rules if read as interest, so frame it as a flat late fee proportionate to administrative/collection cost, not running interest, and do not stack percentage interest on top of a cancellation forfeiture for the same default. Returned/NSF payments stay at $25 first / $35 subsequent (Civ. Code §1719). CPH's current contract already carries a 1%/month interest clause — recommend replacing it with this flat-fee approach. Exact amount and wording pending management + California counsel.

Closing the gaps, realistically. Few private venues share a full contract without a booking, so don't count on that. What actually works: (1) a genuine prospect inquiry or tour — most venues email a proposal/policy sheet or hand over a packet with pricing, deposit and cancellation terms; (2) booking marketplaces (Peerspace, Tagvenue, Giggster) publish cancellation tiers for their listed venues; (3) civic/nonprofit venues (e.g. City Hall Rotunda) publish official rate/deposit/cancellation schedules — use City of San José Event Services, not aggregator figures; (4) event planners and caterers who have worked across these venues have seen their contracts. Accept that some private halls' detailed cancellation and damage clauses will stay unknown — the recommendations here are designed to stand on California law and our own risk, not on copying competitors. Fuller contract-clause, pricing and legal detail is retained in the companion venue-policy-audit-dashboard.
