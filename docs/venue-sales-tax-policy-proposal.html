Headline finding

Renting the hall or courtyard itself is real property — it is not a sale of tangible personal property, so it is generally not subject to California sales tax. The charge these venues most clearly must collect and remit tax on is their own bar / alcoholic-beverage sales. The biggest money risks are therefore the opposite of what many venues assume: over-charging tax on a non-taxable room rental, and under-collecting on bar sales or not paying use tax on owned equipment.

Top compliance risks

  • Mandatory bundled fees (cleaning, coordinator) can be pulled into the taxable measure when the package also rents taxable equipment.
  • Equipment use tax: if tables, linens, A/V, LED, photo booth were bought without paying CA tax, their rental to customers becomes taxable.
  • Bar sales must be taxed and remitted; an ABC license is required.
  • Web CRM vs. mobile app must calculate tax identically.

Decisions management must make (detail in §12)

(1) Confirm whether CA sales/use tax was paid when buying the venues' tables, chairs, linens and A/V equipment. (2) Decide whether cleaning, security and insurance fees are mandatory or optional, and state them separately. (3) Confirm the venue bar is licensed and tax-registered. (4) Approve one written tax policy for both venues. (5) Authorize the CRM tax-engine changes in §08.

Key Findings & Recommendations

California taxes retail sales of tangible personal property (TPP) and leases of TPP. It does not tax real-property rental or stand-alone services. Everything below follows from that rule and the CDTFA Tax Guide for Venue Rental Businesses.

What we found

  1. The room and the courtyard are not taxable. A charge for the use of real property is not a sale or lease of TPP. The base venue/courtyard rental is not subject to sales tax. HIGH
  2. Tables, chairs, linens and equipment are the pivot. These are TPP. Under CDTFA rules, if the venue paid CA sales/use tax when it bought them and rents them in the same form, the charge to the customer is not taxed again. If the venue bought them tax-free (e.g., for resale), the rental charge to the customer is taxable. CONFIRM PURCHASE-TAX STATUS
  3. The venue bar is a taxable retailer. Alcoholic-beverage and other drink/food sales the venue makes are taxable TPP sales and require an ABC license and sales-tax collection. HIGH
  4. Separately stated, optional services are generally not taxable — DJ, security, parking attendants, entertainment — provided the staff do not prepare or serve food/beverages. HIGH (if separately stated)
  5. Mandatory cleaning and mandatory "coordinator" fees are a grey zone. CDTFA treats a mandatory charge tied to a taxable sale/lease as part of the taxable amount; a genuinely optional, separately stated service charge generally is not. CONFIRM
  6. Outside caterers and customer-hired third parties collect their own tax. When the customer pays a caterer, DJ, florist or rental company directly, that vendor — not the venue — is responsible for the tax on its charges. HIGH
  7. Refundable deposits are not taxed when taken — only if/when applied to a taxable charge. HIGH

Recommendations — start now

  • Stop adding sales tax to the bare room/courtyard rental line.
  • Pull every bar/alcohol sale into tax collection and remittance.
  • Separate-state each fee on quotes and invoices with its own taxable / non-taxable flag.
  • Make cleaning, security and insurance genuinely optional where feasible.

Recommendations — confirm, then configure

  • Audit purchase invoices for owned TPP — was CA tax paid? Accrue use tax if not.
  • Get written CDTFA / CPA confirmation on mandatory cleaning & coordinator fees.
  • Confirm the real nature of the "$125 insurance" charge (pass-through premium vs. admin fee).
  • Configure the CRM per §08 before charging tax under the new policy.

08

Proposed Internal Sales Tax Policy

A practical draft for management approval. This is a proposal, not an approved company policy. It applies to both venues and takes effect only after the §12 decisions and CPA/CDTFA confirmation.

A · Pricing & quotations

  • Advertise venue rental as a tax-exclusive price; show sales tax only on genuinely taxable lines.
  • List every component on its own line with a taxable / non-taxable flag — never a single bundled number.
  • Conditional fees (cleaning, insurance) shown with their agreed treatment once confirmed; mark "optional" where that is true.
  • Rate changes are made in one place (the CRM tax table) with an effective date; never hard-coded into quotes.

B · Contracts & agreements

  • Include a sales-tax clause stating tax is added only to taxable items at the rate in effect on the event date (wording in §09).
  • State that deposits are refundable/non-refundable and how a retained deposit is treated.
  • State that customer-hired third-party vendors are responsible for their own taxes and compliance.
  • For any bundled package, itemize the components in the agreement so the non-taxable room rental is distinguishable.

C · Invoices & payment collection

  • Separate non-taxable subtotal, taxable subtotal, and tax; round tax to the cent on the taxable subtotal (not per line).
  • On deposits/partial payments, allocate across taxable and non-taxable lines; collect tax on the taxable portion as it is billed.
  • Refunds and credit notes reverse the original tax proportionally.
  • Final balance reconciles invoice, payments received, tax collected, and deposits held/returned.

D · Accounting & compliance

  • Record collected tax in a Sales Tax Payable liability account — never as revenue.
  • Keep revenue, tax collected, reservation deposits, and refundable security deposits in separate ledger accounts.
  • Reconcile CRM tax reports to the general ledger before each CDTFA return.
  • Retain quotes, contracts, invoices, exemption/resale certificates and purchase invoices for owned TPP for at least 4 years.
  • Assign filing/remittance to a named role; assign monthly review/approval to the accountant.

E · Customer communication

Use plain language: "California sales tax applies only to taxable items such as beverages sold at our bar and certain equipment rentals. The venue rental itself is not subject to sales tax. Any vendors you hire directly bill and handle their own taxes." Never imply every fee is taxed.

F · Policy approval checklist

  • ☐ Purchase-tax status of owned TPP confirmed (accountant).
  • ☐ Cleaning / insurance / coordinator fee treatment confirmed with CPA or CDTFA.
  • ☐ Bar ABC license & seller's permit confirmed active.
  • ☐ Contract & invoice templates updated and legal-reviewed.
  • ☐ CRM configured & tested (§08 CRM specs).
  • ☐ Management sign-off recorded with effective date.

Recommended Contract & Customer Wording

Draft language for legal review — not a substitute for it.

Sales-tax clause (agreement)

"California sales tax is applied only to taxable items (for example, beverages sold by the Venue's bar and certain equipment rentals), at the combined rate in effect for the Venue's location on the event date (currently 10.000% in San Jose). The Venue's rental of the event space is not subject to sales tax. Taxes on goods or services you arrange directly with third-party vendors are the responsibility of those vendors."

Deposit & cancellation clause

"The reservation deposit is applied to your balance and is [non-]refundable. A separate refundable damage/security deposit of $[___] is held and returned after the event, less any documented damages; it is not a payment for goods and is not taxed unless applied to a taxable charge. A cancellation fee, if retained, represents liquidated damages, not payment for goods or services."

Third-party vendor clause

"Vendors you hire directly (caterers, DJs, florists, rental companies, photographers) contract with and invoice you directly and are solely responsible for collecting and remitting any applicable sales tax on their charges."

Bar / alcohol note

"Beverages served by the Venue bar are sold by the Venue under its ABC license. Prices [include / are exclusive of] California sales tax as posted."

Questions for Our CPA / Tax Attorney / CDTFA

The items only a professional or the authority can resolve.

  1. Is a mandatory cleaning fee taxable when the only thing leased is the room (real property), and the venue's tables/linens are tax-paid at purchase?
  2. How should a mandatory "coordinator"/admin fee be treated when the package includes both the non-taxable room and taxable equipment?
  3. For venue-owned tables, chairs, linens, A/V, LED and photo-booth equipment — confirm whether CA tax was paid at purchase, and the correct remedy (use-tax accrual) if not.
  4. Is the digital-only photo booth with attendant a taxable TPP rental or a non-taxable photography service? Does delivering any prints change it?
  5. Is a retained cancellation fee taxable consideration or non-taxable liquidated damages?
  6. Confirm the exact combined rate for each venue address (10.000% vs. any higher straddle-ZIP rate).
  7. Is the $125 "insurance" charge a true premium pass-through (non-taxable) or an in-house admin charge (potentially taxable)?
  8. Does the venue need to register for a seller's permit / ABC license for bar sales, and how should tax-included drink pricing be disclosed?
  9. Treatment of credit-card surcharges added to mixed taxable/non-taxable invoices.
