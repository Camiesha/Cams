D1

Executive summary

The supplied Crown Prince agreement is internally contradictory: it carries two different deposit-and-cancellation models at once, with duplicated and truncated clauses. A separate client-facing promise (a full refund of the initial deposit within two weeks of paying it) appears in neither model. ACD's agreement, reviewed separately, lacks a named contracting entity. None of this is an editing problem — it is a set of business decisions the company must make before either contract is safe to use.

Most significant weaknesses

  • The CPH template contains both the "reservation-deposit-converts-to-security-deposit" model (Version A) and the "separate non-refundable retainer + security deposit" model (Version B). They cannot both govern.
  • The two-week deposit-refund promise (Version C) staff have been making is not written in either version, and the written contract (deposit retained as rescheduling credit / non-refundable retainer) can contradict it.
  • Balance deadline conflict: the written contract says 14 days before the event; operational practice says 30 days with reminders at 30/15/10/5.
  • Overtime rate is blank (a 1.5× fallback is written but no base rate); the no-dispute, 1%/month interest and 3% surcharge clauses need legal review.
  • ACD lacks a named contracting entity and its deposit amount is stated inconsistently ($500 here vs $1,000 in its own supplied agreement).

Decisions management must make first

  1. Which single deposit model governs — A (convert) or B (separate retainer + deposit)?
  2. How the two-week refund promise is honored and written into the contract.
  3. 14-day vs 30-day final-balance deadline.
  4. CPH and ACD deposit amounts ($1,000 / $500 — confirm ACD).
  5. Overtime rate and billing increment for each venue.
  6. ACD contracting entity and payment recipient.

Full list and impacts in the Decision Register (D8).

Most consequential conflict. Under Version A the $1,000 is a reservation deposit that becomes a refundable security deposit and, on cancellation, a rescheduling credit. Under Version B there are two amounts — a non-refundable booking retainer and a separate refundable security deposit. Under Version C, clients have been told the initial deposit is fully refundable within two weeks. A client could reasonably rely on C while the signed contract says A or B. This must be resolved by management decision (D8-01…D8-04) and reviewed by counsel (D9), not chosen silently here.
Proposed standardization. One shared master policy for both venues, with a CPH schedule (D6) and an ACD schedule (D7) carrying the venue-specific amounts, capacity, alcohol handling and courtyard terms. Nothing is transferred from CPH to ACD automatically.

Proposed revised policy (full draft)

PROPOSED DRAFT — FOR MANAGEMENT & CALIFORNIA COUNSEL REVIEW — NOT APPROVED FOR CLIENT USE

A shared master booking policy for both venues. Venue-specific amounts live in the CPH (D6) and ACD (D7) schedules. Bracketed [placeholders] and decision flags mark everything not yet confirmed. Classification chips show whether each clause is a baseline rule, a recommended addition, a proposed change, or a legal-review item.

Crown Prince Hall Corporation (CA Entity No. B20260164272) — Master Event Booking Policy · Version 0.1 (draft) · Effective date: [to be set] · Applies to Crown Prince Event Hall and Alvin Studio & Courtyard per the venue schedule attached to each booking.

1. Scope & definitions

Recommended This policy forms part of your signed booking agreement together with the signed quote/booking summary and the venue-specific schedule. If they conflict, this policy and the venue schedule control over the quote. Reservation payment: the amount that holds your date. Rental charges: the price of the booking. Security deposit: a separate refundable amount held against damage and unpaid charges. Rescheduling credit: a defined credit if you move your date. These four terms are used consistently and are not interchangeable.

2. Reservations & contract formation

Baseline An inquiry or a held date is not a booking. A date is reserved only when the Venue receives your signed agreement and the reservation payment. The Venue may hold a date temporarily for [hold length — confirm the "14-day hold" meaning]; the hold expires if the agreement and payment are not received. Changes require the Venue's written approval. Electronic signatures and emailed notices are accepted.

[MANAGEMENT DECISION REQUIRED] Confirm what the "14-day hold" is — an availability hold before signing, or an unpaid-booking grace period — and its length.

3. Deposits & payment

Proposed change Legal review The company must adopt one deposit model:

Option A (convert): one reservation payment that, after full payment of rental charges, becomes the refundable security deposit; retained as a rescheduling credit on cancellation. Option B (separate): a reservation payment plus a separate refundable security deposit, each accounted for on its own. Recommendation: Option B — cleanest to administer and explain.

[MANAGEMENT DECISION REQUIRED — D8-01] Adopt Option A or Option B. Do not keep both.

Baseline Accepted methods: cash, check, Zelle, credit card. Checks payable to Crown Prince Hall Corporation (ACD: [ACD legal entity]). The final balance is due [14 or 30] days before the event; reminders are sent at 30, 15, 10 and 5 days before. Receipts are issued for every payment; the reservation payment and security deposit are recorded under separate accounting codes and the security deposit is never treated as rental revenue.

[MANAGEMENT DECISION REQUIRED — D8-07] Resolve 14-day (contract) vs 30-day (operational) balance deadline.

4. Cancellation & refunds

Baseline (preserve) Cancellation must be in writing and takes effect when the Venue receives it.

Proposed change Legal review Two-week reservation refund (preserves the current client promise): if you cancel in writing within 14 days after paying your reservation payment, the reservation payment is refunded in full, processed within 14 days. [Confirm this applies to both venues and how the 14 days is counted — D8-03.]

Proposed change After that window, a graduated schedule applies, set to reflect the Venue's actual loss and reviewed by counsel under California law:

  • ≥180 days before: refund all rental payments; retain only the reservation payment or a stated reasonable amount. [%]
  • 90–179 days: retain a defined percentage of rental payments; refund the rest within 30 days. [%]
  • 30–89 days: higher retention; a rescheduling credit offered first. [%]
  • <30 days: committed costs + a reasonable estimate; no stacking a flat amount on a full-payment retention. [%]

Baseline (preserve) The Venue may not recover the same loss twice. The security deposit is returned in every case, less documented deductions. Refunds are processed within 30 days; the security deposit within 14 days.

[MANAGEMENT DECISION REQUIRED — D8-06 · LEGAL] Set the cancellation percentages with counsel. The current "keep everything inside 180 days" term is the highest enforceability risk.

5. Rescheduling & postponement

Baseline (preserve) You may move your date once to any available date, subject to the new date's pricing, by completing a replacement booking confirmed in writing within one year. A request alone is not a completed booking; further changes do not restart the deadline. Unused credit may be forfeited after one year, to the extent permitted by law. A rescheduling credit is offered before any cash forfeiture.

6. Security deposit, damage & cleaning

Baseline (preserve) The security deposit is held against damage, missing items, clogged plumbing, excessive cleaning, trash, overtime, unpaid charges and fines caused by you, your guests or vendors. The Venue documents deductions with photos/invoices and sends an itemized statement within 14 days. The deposit is security only and does not cap what you owe. The Venue is not responsible for lost or stolen property.

7. Access, event duration & overtime

Baseline Your booked time includes load-in, setup, event, cleanup, breakdown and load-out. Proposed change CPH: a 6-hour event window plus 2 complimentary setup hours before it (aligning the contract with the rate card and website). No early access or late stay without written approval.

Proposed change Overtime is billed at [$ per hour] per hour or part, after a [grace, e.g. 15-min] grace period, settled at the final walkthrough; if no rate is stated, the fallback is 1.5× the hourly rental rate.

[MANAGEMENT DECISION REQUIRED — D8-11] Supply the overtime rate and grace period for each venue; verify the 1.5× fallback is approved policy.

8. Guest capacity & venue use

Baseline (preserve) Guest count may not exceed the lower of legal occupancy and Venue-approved capacity. No smoking/vaping except in the designated outdoor area; no cannabis on the property. No illegal drugs, weapons, fighting, harassment, blocked exits, gambling, fireworks, pyrotechnics, or open flames/candles/fog/haze/glitter/confetti without written approval. No pets except service animals required by law. Nothing attached to walls, ceilings, floors or fixtures without written approval. Keep accessible entrances, routes, restrooms and parking clear. Differentiate Venue rules from requirements imposed by law, permit, landlord or insurer.

9. Alcohol

Baseline Alcohol is allowed only with written approval; the Venue does not sell, furnish or serve alcohol unless a signed add-on says otherwise. You are responsible for lawful service — no service to minors or visibly intoxicated persons, licensed/authorized servers where required, drinking only in approved areas, never in the parking lot. No sale, cash bar, donations, or ticketed drinks unless your caterer holds a valid California ABC catering license for the date and gives the Venue a copy at least 10 days before. Baseline Where alcohol is served, licensed security is required — CPH: one guard per 75 guests, minimum two. Info missing ACD: [ratio to confirm].

10. Catering, vendors & production

Baseline (preserve) Food, caterers, food trucks, DJs, performers, rentals, décor and production must be disclosed and approved before the event. Caterers/food trucks must hold a current Santa Clara County health permit for the date and give the Venue a copy at least 10 days before. Every vendor must carry liability insurance and provide a certificate at least 30 days before. You are responsible for your vendors. Confirm "The Venue does not accept, store or secure deliveries" — retain only if it matches actual operations.

11. Insurance, liability & indemnification

Baseline (CPH) You must carry event liability insurance of at least $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate, plus $1,000,000 host liquor liability if alcohol is served. Crown Prince Hall Corporation and its landlord must be named additional insureds; a certificate is due at least 30 days before. Info missing (ACD) ACD limits/additional insureds [to set]. Legal review The release (incl. Civil Code §1542 waiver), indemnification and limitation of liability are preserved as drafted but must be narrowed to exclude gross negligence, willful misconduct and non-waivable liability, and confirmed by counsel.

12. Safety, compliance & event termination

Baseline (preserve) The Venue, security, police, fire officials or authorities may deny entry, remove persons, stop alcohol service, reduce sound, close areas, suspend or end the event for safety, compliance, property protection, non-payment, illegal activity, intoxication, violence, damage, capacity or material breach. Legal review Distinguish immediate safety interventions from ordinary breaches that can be cured; review the "no refund if ended for breach" term for proportionality.

13. Photography, video & privacy

Baseline (preserve) Marketing use is limited to non-identifying images of setups, décor and atmosphere; no image identifying a guest, family member or staff is published without written consent. Opt-in/opt-out is provided; if neither box is checked, the Venue will not use images for marketing. This does not restrict security footage, incident documentation or legally required records.

14. Force majeure & venue-initiated cancellation

Baseline If circumstances beyond either party's control make it illegal or impossible to provide or use the facilities, either party may terminate on written notice as soon as practical and no later than 10 days after learning of the basis. Proposed change The primary remedy is a credit or reschedule; refund treatment of the reservation payment is [confirm — currently non-refundable]. Recommended addition If the Venue cancels for a reason that is not your fault, you receive a full refund of all amounts paid and help finding an alternative date or venue.

15. Accessibility (Civil Code §1938)

Info missing Legal review The §1938 CASp disclosure must state the actual inspection status for each premises. [CPH status — confirm] · [ACD status — confirm]. Do not select a box without verified information for that premises.

16. General & signatures

Baseline California law governs. The parties will attempt mediation in Santa Clara County before suit; any lawsuit is filed in Santa Clara County Superior Court; the prevailing party may recover reasonable attorney fees. Entire-agreement, written-amendments and severability apply. Legal review The limitation of the Venue's total responsibility to amounts paid is preserved but counsel must confirm. The signer is at least 18 (21 if alcohol is served) and, if signing for a company, is personally responsible for amounts owed. Client acknowledgment and initials fields are retained per section.

Internal implementation plan

Phased rollout. Client-facing terms (what clients must do) are kept separate from internal staff procedures (how staff administer them). CRM items are recommendations to confirm against the actual configuration, not assumed to exist.

Phased rollout

  1. Management decisions (D8) — deposit model, refund promise, deadlines, amounts, overtime, ACD entity.
  2. Legal review (D9) — cancellation, waivers, surcharge, interest, alcohol, CASp.
  3. Contract & quote updates — rebuild a single clean template (one deposit model, filled fields); align the quote.
  4. Website & client communication — match policy; publish the two-week refund window.
  5. CRM configuration — fields, reminders, separate deposit accounting, document gates.
  6. Staff training — one script for deposits, refunds and the two-week window.
  7. Effective date & transition — new terms apply to new bookings only; existing bookings keep their terms.
  8. Consistency audit (D11) before client use.

  • Balance due date; reminders 30/15/10/5
