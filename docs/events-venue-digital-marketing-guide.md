# Events Venue Digital Marketing — Training Guide

**Context:** Crown Prince Event Hall and Alvin Studio (San Jose, CA)
**Audience:** Anyone who needs to understand the marketing system well enough to explain it in a meeting.
**Scope:** All event types — corporate, parties, community, cultural, private bookings. Not wedding-specific.

> **Pricing rule that applies everywhere in this guide:** no venue or package prices are published on the website, in ads, or in the brochure. Pricing is **custom quote only**, given after we understand the event. The brochure sells the space and the experience; the quote comes later.

> **Numbers in this guide are illustrative examples**, used to show how the math works. Real numbers come from the actual accounts.

---

## 1. The Big Picture

The whole system is one path a stranger walks from "searching on Google" to "signed booking":

```
SEO + Google Ads
        ↓
Website / Landing Page
        ↓
"Get Event Brochure" request
        ↓
Lead
        ↓
CRM
        ↓
Follow-Up (email / SMS / call)
        ↓
Qualified Lead
        ↓
Venue Tour
        ↓
Custom Quote
        ↓
Booking
```

Three jobs, in order:

| Stage | Job | Tools |
|---|---|---|
| **CONNECT** | Get the right people to see us | SEO + Google Ads |
| **CONVERT** | Turn visitors into leads | Landing page + brochure request |
| **CLOSE** | Turn leads into bookings | CRM + follow-up + qualification + tour + custom quote |

Every stage loses some people. That is normal. The work is to lose fewer at each step and to know **where** we are losing them.

## 2. SEO (Search Engine Optimization)

### What SEO is
SEO is the work of making our website show up in Google's **free** (unpaid) results when someone searches for a venue like ours. We don't pay Google per click. We pay in time, content, and setup.

### How Google Search works — in three steps
1. **Crawl** — Google's software visits pages on the internet and reads them.
2. **Index** — Google stores what each page is about.
3. **Rank** — When someone searches, Google orders the pages it thinks are most relevant and trustworthy for that search, in that person's location.

We can't control the ranking. We can control how clearly our pages answer a search, and how much trust the business has built.

### Keywords and search intent
- **Keyword** = the words someone types into Google.
- **Search intent** = what they actually want.

Same topic, very different intent:

| Search | Intent | Worth our money? |
|---|---|---|
| "what is an event venue" | Just learning | No |
| "events venue San Jose" | Looking for a venue now | **Yes** |
| "corporate event venue San Jose" | Ready to inquire, knows the event type | **Yes — highest value** |
| "event venue jobs San Jose" | Looking for work | No |

Chasing high-intent searches is the entire game. A page that ranks for a low-intent search brings traffic that never books.

### Organic traffic
**Organic traffic** = visitors who arrive from the free search results, not from an ad. It builds slowly, then keeps arriving without paying per click. It is the compounding asset.

### Local SEO
A venue is a physical place, so most of our searches are local — people looking in San Jose and the surrounding area. Local SEO is what makes us appear in the **map pack** (the map with three business listings that appears above normal results).

Local SEO depends heavily on:
- A complete, active **Google Business Profile** (hours, photos, categories, service area, event types)
- **NAP consistency** — the exact same Name, Address, Phone everywhere online
- **Reviews** — volume, recency, and replies
- Real photos of the actual space

### Example searches we want to own
- "events venue San Jose"
- "party venue San Jose"
- "corporate event venue San Jose"
- plus variations: "event space San Jose," "banquet hall San Jose," "venue rental near me," "birthday party venue San Jose," "photo studio rental San Jose" (Alvin Studio)

### What an events venue should do to improve SEO
1. **One strong page per event type** — corporate events, parties, community/cultural events, studio rental. Not one page trying to be everything.
2. **Say the location out loud** — San Jose, the neighborhoods, the region, in real sentences, not stuffed lists.
3. **Google Business Profile, kept alive** — photos, posts, correct hours, every event category we serve.
4. **Reviews, continuously** — ask every completed event; reply to all of them.
5. **Real photos and floor plans** — capacity, layouts, parking, A/V. This is what people actually search for and what keeps them on the page.
6. **Fast, mobile-first pages** — most venue searching happens on a phone.
7. **Answer real questions on the site** — capacity, parking, catering rules, hours, accessibility. These pages earn rankings and reduce back-and-forth later.
8. **Local links and listings** — chamber of commerce, event directories, local partners, vendors.

SEO is slow. Expect months, not weeks. That is exactly why we also run ads.

## 3. Google Ads

### What Google Ads is
Google Ads is how we **pay** to appear at the top of search results. We bid to show for chosen searches, and we're charged when someone clicks. It turns on today and produces leads this week.

### Search Ads
The text ads at the top of Google results, marked "Sponsored." Someone searches "corporate event venue San Jose," our ad appears above the free results, they click, they land on our page. This is the ad type that matters most for a venue, because the person is already looking.

### Keywords vs. search terms — the most important distinction
- **Keyword** = what *we* tell Google we want to show up for.
- **Search term** = what the person *actually typed*.

They are not the same. We bid on "party venue San Jose," and Google may show our ad for "cheap party venue," "party venue jobs," or "party supplies San Jose." The **Search Terms Report** shows the real typed searches. Reading it regularly is the single highest-value habit in an ads account.

### Campaigns and ad groups — how an account is organized
```
Account
 └─ Campaign  (budget + location + goal, e.g. "Corporate Events – San Jose")
     └─ Ad Group  (one tight theme, e.g. "corporate event venue")
         ├─ Keywords
         └─ Ads → point to the matching landing page
```
Keep it tight: one theme per ad group, and the ad and landing page should say the same thing the person searched. Someone who searched "corporate event venue" should land on the corporate page, not the homepage.

### The core metrics
| Term | Meaning | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **CPC** — Cost Per Click | What we pay for one click | Spend ÷ Clicks |
| **CTR** — Click-Through Rate | % of people who saw the ad and clicked | Clicks ÷ Impressions |
| **Budget** | The daily/monthly cap we set per campaign | — |
| **Conversion** | The action we count as valuable (a brochure request) | — |
| **CPL** — Cost Per Lead | What one lead costs us | Spend ÷ Leads |

### Negative keywords
**Negative keywords** tell Google what *not* to show us for. They stop wasted spend. For a venue, obvious ones:

`free, cheap, jobs, hiring, salary, DIY, rental equipment, tables and chairs, wedding dress, party supplies, [other cities we don't serve]`

Every review of the Search Terms Report should produce a few more negatives. This is how CPL comes down without touching the budget.

### Simple example (illustrative)
> Campaign: **Corporate Events – San Jose**, $40/day
> Ad group: "corporate event venue"
> Keywords: corporate event venue San Jose, corporate event space San Jose, company party venue San Jose
> Negatives: free, cheap, jobs, hotel, catering supplies
> Ad headline: "Corporate Event Venue in San Jose — Request the Event Brochure"
> Landing page: the corporate events page, with the brochure form at the top
>
> **Month result:** $1,200 spent → 600 clicks ($2.00 CPC) → 30 brochure requests (5% conversion rate) → **$40 CPL**. Of those 30, 12 qualified, 6 toured, 2 booked → **$600 cost per booking**.

Whether $600 per booking is good depends entirely on what a booking is worth to us — that's the accounting question that decides the budget.

## 4. SEO vs. Google Ads

| | SEO | Google Ads |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Time and content | Money per click |
| Speed | Months | Same day |
| Traffic when you stop | Keeps coming | Stops immediately |
| Control | Indirect | Direct — exact searches, budget, timing |
| Best at | Long-term, lower cost per lead | Speed, testing, filling slow months |

**Why a venue needs both:**
- Ads produce leads **now** and tell us in days which searches actually convert.
- SEO produces leads **cheaper over time** and keeps producing when we pause spending.
- Ads are a **testing lab for SEO**: the search terms that convert in ads are exactly the pages worth building for organic.
- Ads let us push hard in slow booking months and pull back in busy ones. SEO can't be turned up on demand.

Running only ads means renting all our traffic forever. Running only SEO means waiting months with no pipeline. Both, together.

## 5. CONVERT — Google Traffic → Landing Page → "Get Event Brochure"

### What a landing page does
A **landing page** is the page a visitor lands on after clicking. It has **one job**: get the brochure request. No menu maze, no distractions, no five competing links. A homepage is a lobby; a landing page is a doorway.

What belongs on a venue landing page:
- Headline that matches the search ("Corporate Event Venue in San Jose")
- Real photos of the space
- Capacity, layouts, parking, location
- Proof: reviews, past events, recognizable clients or event types
- **One clear offer:** Get the Event Brochure
- Phone number, tap-to-call, visible on mobile

### CTA (Call To Action)
The **CTA** is the button and the words on it. One primary CTA, repeated down the page: **"Get the Event Brochure."** Not "Submit." Not "Learn more." Say what they get.

### Lead form
The **lead form** is what turns an anonymous visitor into a contact we can follow up with. Keep it short — every extra field loses people.

Recommended fields:
- Name
- Email
- Phone
- Event type (dropdown: corporate, party, community/cultural, studio/photo, other)
- Event date (or "not sure yet")
- Estimated guest count

Those last three are the qualification questions, gathered for free at the moment they're most willing to answer.

### Conversion
A **conversion** is a completed valuable action. For us the primary conversion is a **brochure request**. Secondary conversions worth tracking: phone calls, tour bookings.

**Conversion rate = Leads ÷ Visitors.** If 600 people visit and 30 request the brochure, that's 5%.

### The brochure
The brochure is the thing we trade for their contact details. It should make them want to see the space in person.

**Include:** photos of every space and layout, capacities, floor plans, what's included, A/V and amenities, parking and access, event types we host, catering and vendor policy, sample timelines, reviews, and a clear next step ("Book a tour").

**Do NOT include: any prices, package rates, or price ranges.** Pricing is a custom quote based on date, guest count, hours, and setup. The brochure ends with an invitation to a tour and a quote — not a price list.

Why: dates, seasons, hours, and setups vary too much for a fixed number to be accurate, a printed price invites comparison-shopping on price alone, and the quote is the conversation where we actually sell the value.

## 6. CLOSE — Brochure → Response → Follow-Up → Qualification → Tour → Quote → Booking

### Automated vs. human follow-up

**Automated** (fires by itself, seconds after the form):
- Instant email with the brochure attached or linked
- Instant SMS: "Thanks {Name} — your Crown Prince event brochure is on its way. Want to see the space? Here's the tour calendar."
- A scheduled sequence of reminders over the following days

**Human** (a person, with judgment):
- The call or personal message that qualifies the lead
- The tour itself
- The custom quote and the negotiation

Automation buys us **speed and consistency**. Humans **close**. Automation should never be the last touch on a real lead.

### Speed matters
Leads go cold fast. Someone requesting a venue brochure is very likely requesting from three venues in the same sitting. The first venue to respond meaningfully has a large advantage.

> **On the "37-minute follow-up":** the playbook's 37-minute figure is **an example to test, not a fixed rule.** The principle is real — respond fast. The exact number should be tested against our own data (try 5 minutes, 37 minutes, and 2 hours, and compare which produces more tours). Don't defend the number; defend the principle.

### CRM
A **CRM** (Customer Relationship Management system) is the single place where every lead lives, along with their details, event type, date, source, every message sent, and their current stage. Without it, leads sit in an inbox and quietly die.

Every lead should carry: **source** (SEO, Google Ads, referral, walk-in), event type, event date, guest count, stage, owner, next action, and next action date.

The source field is what makes the whole measurement chain possible — without it we can never say which channel produced a booking.

### Lead stages
```
New          → form just submitted
Contacted    → we responded; no reply yet
Qualified    → real event, real date, fits our space and calendar
Tour Booked  → tour on the calendar
Toured       → they've seen the space
Quoted       → custom quote sent
Booked       → contract signed / deposit paid
Lost         → with a reason recorded
```

**Qualification** = confirming this is a real, workable event: is there a date, is the date available, does the guest count fit the space, is the event type one we host, and are they the decision maker. A lead that fails qualification isn't a failure — it's a lead we stop spending time on, and a signal about our targeting.

Always record a **lost reason** (price, date unavailable, capacity, went silent, chose competitor). Lost reasons are the cheapest marketing research available.

## 7. How Everything Integrates

```
Google Ads + SEO          ← bring the right people
        ↓
Website / Landing Page    ← one job: earn the brochure request
        ↓
Tracking / GA4            ← records what happened, and where it came from
        ↓
Lead Form                 ← the visitor becomes a contact
        ↓
CRM                       ← one home for every lead + its source
        ↓
Email / SMS Follow-Up     ← instant response, then a sequence
        ↓
Venue Tour Calendar       ← self-serve booking, synced to staff calendars
        ↓
Custom Quote              ← built after the tour, from real details
        ↓
Booking                   ← contract + deposit, marked back in the CRM
```

**Tracking / GA4** deserves its own note. GA4 (Google Analytics 4) is the measurement layer that records what visitors do on the site and which channel sent them. Together with conversion tracking in Google Ads, it's what lets us connect *this booking* back to *that keyword*.

The two connections people most often forget to build:
1. **Ads → GA4 → CRM source field.** If the lead's source isn't captured automatically at form submission, every cost-per-booking number becomes guesswork.
2. **Booking → back into the CRM.** If signed contracts are never marked in the CRM, we'll optimize toward leads instead of toward revenue — and cheap leads that never book will look like success.

A chain is only as good as its weakest link. A great ad feeding a slow landing page wastes money. A great landing page with no follow-up wastes leads. A great follow-up with no tour calendar wastes attention.

## 8. Important Metrics

The funnel, top to bottom, with the formula and why it matters:

| Metric | Formula | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| **Impressions** | count | How often our ad or listing appeared. Reach — a visibility number, not a performance one. |
| **Clicks** | count | How many actually came to the site. |
| **CTR** (Click-Through Rate) | Clicks ÷ Impressions | Whether the ad and search match. Low CTR = wrong keywords or weak ad copy. |
| **CPC** (Cost Per Click) | Spend ÷ Clicks | The price of one visitor. Rising CPC = more competition or lower ad quality. |
| **Leads** | count of brochure requests | The first real business outcome. |
| **Conversion Rate** | Leads ÷ Clicks (or Visitors) | How well the landing page works. Low here = page problem, not a traffic problem. |
| **CPL** (Cost Per Lead) | Spend ÷ Leads | What one lead costs. The main efficiency dial in ads. |
| **Qualified Leads** | count of leads that pass qualification | Lead **quality**. Many leads but few qualified = targeting problem. |
| **Tours** | count of tours held | The strongest predictor of a booking. This is the number to grow. |
| **Bookings** | count of signed contracts | The actual result. |
| **Cost Per Booking** | Total Spend ÷ Bookings | The number that decides whether the marketing is worth doing. |

### Worked example (illustrative)
```
Spend                $1,200
Impressions          20,000
Clicks                  600   → CTR 3.0%,  CPC $2.00
Leads                    30   → Conversion rate 5.0%,  CPL $40
Qualified leads          12   → 40% of leads
Tours                     6   → 50% of qualified
Bookings                  2   → 33% of tours
                              → Cost per booking $600
```

**How to read it:** each ratio isolates one part of the system.
- Low CTR → the **ads/keywords** need work
- Low conversion rate → the **landing page** needs work
- Low qualified % → the **targeting** is attracting the wrong events
- Low tours from qualified → the **follow-up or tour booking** is the leak
- Low bookings from tours → the **tour experience or quote** is the leak

You cannot fix a funnel you only measure at the ends. Measure every step, find the one weakest ratio, fix that one.

## 9. Implementation Plan

Do these in order. Each step is useless without the one before it.

**1. Audit current campaigns and funnel**
What Google Ads campaigns exist, what they spend, which keywords convert. Walk the funnel yourself as a customer, on a phone: search → click → land → submit the form → see what actually arrives.

**2. Check tracking**
Confirm GA4 is installed, the brochure request is set up as a conversion, Google Ads and GA4 are linked, and phone calls are tracked. **Nothing after this step can be measured if this step is broken — and this is where most venues are broken.**

**3. Improve or build the landing page**
One page per major event type, one CTA, real photos, capacity and parking answered, fast on mobile. No prices.

**4. Set up brochure lead capture**
Short form, instant delivery, brochure with no pricing, ending in a tour invitation.

**5. Connect the CRM**
Every lead lands in the CRM automatically with its source, event type, date, and guest count. No manual copying from email.

**6. Build follow-up**
Instant automated email + SMS, then a scheduled sequence, then a human call. Test response timing rather than assuming a number.

**7. Connect tour booking**
A self-serve tour calendar linked from the brochure, the follow-up emails, and the landing page. Synced to real staff availability, with automatic reminders (no-shows are pure waste).

**8. Measure results**
A single monthly report showing the full chain: spend → impressions → clicks → leads → qualified → tours → bookings → cost per booking, broken out by source.

**9. Optimize**
Every month: read the Search Terms Report, add negative keywords, pause the keywords that produce leads but no tours, test one landing page change and one follow-up change at a time.

**10. Scale**
Only after cost per booking is known and acceptable, increase budget on what already works and expand into more event types and nearby areas. **Never scale spend on a funnel that isn't measured** — that just buys more of an unknown.

## Questions to Ask Leon

Ordered so the answers build a picture of what exists versus what still needs building.

**What's running now**
1. Which Google Ads campaigns are live right now, what's the monthly budget on each, and which event types do they target?
2. Is conversion tracking actually working — is the brochure request set up as a conversion in Google Ads, and is GA4 linked to the Ads account?
3. What is our current cost per lead, and do we know cost per **booking** — or only cost per lead?

**The conversion step**
4. Do ads point to a dedicated landing page per event type, or to the homepage? And what's the conversion rate on that page?
5. What exactly does someone receive after requesting the brochure, how fast does it arrive, and can you confirm it contains no pricing?

**The close step**
6. Which CRM are we using, do leads arrive in it automatically with their source attached, and what lead stages are set up?
7. What follow-up is automated today versus done by a person — and what's our actual average response time to a new lead?
8. How does someone book a tour: self-serve calendar, or back-and-forth by phone and email? What's our tour no-show rate?

**Direction**
9. Where do we lose the most people — clicks that don't become leads, leads that never qualify, or tours that don't book?
10. Of everything on the implementation plan, what's already built, and what's the first missing piece you'd build next?

*(Question 2 is the one to press hardest. If tracking is broken, every other number in this guide is a guess.)*
