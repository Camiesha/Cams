OrientationHow to use this playbook

Every scenario separates three things. Read them differently.

What you say

Green Say blocks are word-for-word spoken lines. Read them aloud or paraphrase naturally. Bracketed items like [Client Name] or [Event Date] are placeholders you fill from the lead.

What you do

Grey guidance, CRM, and Verify first blocks are internal only. Never read them to a client. The red Verify first line lists what must be confirmed before you promise anything.

Golden rule. Published starting prices are not final quotes. Availability, tour times, quote amounts, and bookings are never confirmed on a call — they are confirmed only after the authorized team checks the schedule, reviews requirements, and verifies payment. When unsure, say you'll confirm and follow up. Never invent a policy, price, discount, or deposit figure.

CRM pipeline stages used throughout follow the venue's live Web CRM: Lead Contacted Tour Proposal Won. Where a specific sub-status (e.g. a quote or payment state) hasn't been standardized, the playbook says "use the corresponding approved CRM status" rather than inventing one.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confirming a date, tour time, or booking before it's actually checked/verified.
  • Quoting a final total or stating a deposit, tax, extra-hour, or add-on figure from memory.
  • Implying every service is included in the starting package.
  • Inventing a cancellation, refund, or payment policy instead of verifying.
  • Promising a discount or approving a policy exception yourself.
  • Mentioning a courtyard or studio — Crown Prince is one hall; those belong to another venue.
  • Treating a claimed payment, verbal yes, or unsigned document as a confirmed booking.
  • Reading internal CRM notes to the client, or leaving discovery as an interrogation.
  • Letting a lead go cold with no logged next action and follow-up date.
