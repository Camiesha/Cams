Section 05

Standard discovery flow

Collect these naturally over the conversation — not as a checklist read-out. Explain why you're asking when it isn't obvious, and never re-ask something the client already told you.

The natural order
  • Warm open & event type — "What are we celebrating?" tells you almost everything about tone.
  • Date & alternatives — "Do you have a date in mind, or a few that could work?"
  • Guest count — ties to capacity (up to 300) and whether you're past the 100-guest starting package.
  • Times & setup/cleanup — "Roughly what start and end time? Will you need setup time beforehand?"
  • Floor plan / seating — banquet, theater, cocktail, ceremony, or combined ceremony + reception.
  • Budget range — only if comfortable: "So I can point you the right direction, do you have a budget in mind?"
  • Catering & bar — "Bringing your own caterer? Would you like a bar package?"
  • DJ / entertainment / AV — stage, dance floor, lighting, big-screen TVs, mics.
  • Decor & customization — florals, backdrops, VIP entrance, marquee numbers, prep room.
  • Anything affecting suitability — accessibility, timing restrictions, cultural traditions.
  • Contact + tour preference — best phone/email and a tour day/time that suits them.

Section 06

Objection-handling framework

Same five steps every time. The goal is never to "win" — it's to understand the real concern and keep the conversation moving.

StepDo thisSounds like
1. AcknowledgeName the concern out loud"That's a completely fair thing to weigh."
2. EmpathizeShow you're on their side"Planning a celebration is a big investment — I get it."
3. ClarifyFind the real issue underneath"When you say the price feels high, is it the total, or the fit for what you need?"
4. OfferGive a relevant solution or next step"A weekday date starts lower at $3,845, and seeing the space usually makes the value clearer."
5. AskA simple question that continues"Would a weekday work for your event?"

Price objection

Acknowledge → clarify total vs. fit → point to the weekday starting rate and all-included items (10 tables for 100, security, cleaning, insurance, gated parking) → invite a tour → "What date range are you working with?" Never promise a discount.

Competitor comparison

Never criticize the other venue. → clarify what matters most → highlight what's included and the stage/dance-floor/VIP-room flexibility → "Would it help to see it in person before you decide?"

Indecision

Acknowledge it's a big choice → clarify what's holding them → offer a no-pressure tour → "Would it help to walk the space with no obligation?"

Scheduling concern

Acknowledge → clarify the constraint → offer alternative dates or flexible tour times (7 days/week) → "Would a weekday tour be easier?" Never promise availability before checking.

Budget limitation

Acknowledge → clarify the ceiling → steer toward weekday pricing → offer a quote built around priorities → "If I put together options around that range, would that help?"

Guest-count concern

Acknowledge → confirm count → note the hall seats up to 300 and the starting package covers 100 (more tables quoted) → "What count should I plan around?" Over 300 → capacity review.

Package-inclusion question

Acknowledge → confirm what's included (tables for 100, security, cleaning, insurance) vs. add-ons → "Which pieces matter most so I get your quote right?" Don't imply everything is bundled.

Special arrangement / discount request

Acknowledge → clarify exactly what they need → document and refer to the authorized decision-maker, don't promise → "Let me confirm what we can do and get right back to you — what's the best number?"
Never: pressure the client, claim a date is available without checking, criticize a competitor, or promise a discount, exception, or policy you aren't authorized to confirm.

  • 3 Clarify the real issue
  • 4 Offer a solution / next step

  • Confirm the agreed next step
  • Set a specific follow-up time
  • Default close = book the tour
  • Recap what you'll verify
  • Thank them by name

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Confirming a date, tour time, or booking before it's actually checked/verified.
  • Quoting a final total or stating a deposit, tax, extra-hour, or add-on figure from memory.
  • Implying every service is included in the starting package.
  • Inventing a cancellation, refund, or payment policy instead of verifying.
  • Promising a discount or approving a policy exception yourself.
  • Mentioning a courtyard or studio — Crown Prince is one hall; those belong to another venue.
  • Treating a claimed payment, verbal yes, or unsigned document as a confirmed booking.
  • Reading internal notes to the client, or letting discovery feel like an interrogation.
  • Letting a lead go cold with no logged next action and follow-up date.
